2

Where you're losing time

The Knowledge Flow Audit. Don't answer in general — trace one real thing.

Somebody at your firm has already run the experiment: fed a spec section and a few drawings into a chatbot, got back something fluent and mostly right. Then ran it across ten more and the results scattered — strong on one, wrong on the next, differently wrong on the third.

The failure wasn't the model. It was that there was no structure underneath it. AI can only retrieve what you captured, and it will confidently reuse what you don't trust. This worksheet is where you find out whether you have anything to retrieve.

Where did they look first?
How long until they had an answer?
Where did the answer actually live?
After it was answered, where did the answer go?
If that person left tomorrow, does the answer survive?

Now score that chain

1 is the floor, 5 is the ceiling. Score what's true, not what's on the website.

Ownership
1 · It lives in a vendor's platform on their terms5 · You could take it and leave tomorrow

Why Ownership is on this list. The other four rows are internal hygiene. This one is strategic. McKinsey's test for when data becomes an advantage is four-part: captured as the work happens, structured for reuse, traceable to origin, and held with the rights to keep learning from it. That fourth clause is the one most firms already answered years ago, in software contracts nobody re-read. It applies to every vendor in this room.
